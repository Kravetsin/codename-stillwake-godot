# Fog at a planet's edges

- Status: accepted; built in four PRs (bands of bedrock outside the zone; the bands hollow; the glide, the move, the
  fog; the fog thick past the bands, textured)
- Date: 2026-10-26

## Context

A planet (0077) ended at a column of unbreakable bedrock at each side, on the surface and underground, and past it the
camera showed nothing. The user wanted the planet to feel endless without a world that wraps: wrapping or streaming the
grid would touch every module's cell arithmetic (some 250 places), the lockstep, saves and the generator, for little
play, since a planet is already minutes across and its ruins are counted.

## Decision

**Each side of a planet is a band of fog the hero glides through, and at its middle it is moved, unseen, to the other
side's band.** The sim stays a finite grid; the glide and the move are the client's, as all hero movement is (0002).

- **The band, in the sim:** `Ground.Fog` columns at each side (64 on the first planet, 8 on the small), outside the build
  zone (`Layout.Zone`, 0048), so nothing is planned there. `Terrain` grows them hollow: the open sky over their ground
  line, open air under it to the core before a bedrock back wall, dark and unbreakable as a cave's, so a hit finds
  nothing to break. No landmark, ruin, vein or growth is in a band; no cave mouth opens within a band's width of one;
  cave growth keeps a column off them. Creatures find every column outside a planet's zone solid: none goes in or
  spawns there. Nothing new is saved; a grown grid already saves its zone.
- **The glide, in the client:** while the hero's body centre is out of the zone, no gravity: its height is kept, up,
  down, jump and ladders do nothing, left and right pace it as on the ground. It is drawn floating. Entering the glide
  lets go of the harpoon. A hero moved across glides on over the `Ground.FogOut` columns past the band, where the fog
  is still thick, and only then falls, so it is seen dropping out of the fog; one walking in from the zone walks there.
- **The move:** each band's *seam* is a vertical line at its middle; crossing one outward moves the hero by the period
  between them to the other seam, at `Ground.FogDrop` rows over the highest ground it will glide on over: that band's
  line beside the zone and the `FogOut` columns past it as they stand, towers built there included. Its speed across
  is kept. A seated hero is never moved. The camera snaps, and the hero's cell is reported that frame. Other peers snap a hero
  that jumps far (`Remote.Snap`).
- **One source:** `FogEdges.Of(grid, rules)` reads the bands from the zone; a planet whose zone is the whole grid (saved
  before the bands) has none. `FogShare` says how thick the fog is by column and over the screen.
- **The fog** is a screen layer over the whole world, friends and the hero's own included, under all that is read:
  whole over each band, so its hollow is never seen, thinning over the `FogOut` columns past it to where a moved hero
  falls out, and to nothing a little further in; the whole screen whole at the seam, and held whole a moment at a move.
  Textured from the frost's noise (billows, flat wisps), never a flat colour; the texture runs on across a move. Under
  the ground line it darkens as underground is, the less near a seam. In the fog the World bus is muffled as in space.

## Consequences

- A hero can enter the fog at any height, from the surface or a tunnel, and always leaves the far band from over the
  ground. Turning back before a seam floats it back out at its own height.
- Creatures, stacks and thrown items never cross. A stack, or a passed-out hero's carried load, in a band falls to the
  core. A hero in a band is out of walkers' reach.
- A wall built past a band raises the drop height with it; one built after the hero is moved, across its glide, stops
  it: it breaks it or turns back.
- Planets saved before the bands keep a full-grid zone: no fog, no glide, and a band to fall into. Planets are reached
  only from the dev panel until the drop pod's launch slice; that slice says what becomes of older saves. A later change
  of `Fog` leaves saved planets with their old zone.
- Not handled: air in the fog (the suit breathes as on the planet), markers or sound across the seam, the camera's lead
  across it.
