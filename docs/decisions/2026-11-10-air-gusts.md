# An action stirs its surroundings

- Status: accepted
- Date: 2026-11-10

## Context

Striking and shooting needed weight. Effects drawn on the weapon itself were tried first: an arm swept round the body, a
smear and a fan off the blade. The sweep lost the arm as the flat cutouts turned edge-on, and the user found the smears
did not read. The user's idea was to make the surroundings answer instead. An axe's stroke blows the dust beneath it. A
railgun's shot is heavy like a cannon's and kicks the floor's dust and grit out round the shooter.

## Decision

- **A gust is a push of air** (`Gust`, in cells): a point, a way or every way, a strength (1 a railgun's shot) and a
  radius. Each action that pushes air throws one gust as it happens. A swung tool throws one as its stroke lands, from where
  the blade first touches, else from the end of its reach, away from the shoulder. A railgun throws one round the feet and
  one along the barrel from the muzzle. The strengths are data (`GustStrengths`): the sledgehammer 0.75, the axe 0.5, the
  pickaxe 0.45, the spear 0.3, the railgun's feet 1 and its muzzle 0.8.
- **The surroundings answer, not the tool** (`Gusts.Throw`). The gust looks only at the cells within its radius, once.
  - Each column's floor, felt as if from half as high, raises dust motes, grains and a rolling veil away from the gust.
    The dust takes the ground's own colour, and a built floor's is the hull's grey.
  - A stroke up in the air raises nothing.
  - Where a cell floats in no air there is no dust.
  - From a strong gust (0.35 and up, a heavy action, never a light stroke), the ceilings' undersides and open walls near it
    shed grit, floating where the cell is afloat.
- **Over the walls.** The dust is drawn over the walls, not behind them as a landing's is, so it rises over the grass.
- **No ring of light.** A railgun's shot sent a flat ring out along the floor; the user took it out in play, as they did the
  landing's ([body-answers](2026-11-10-body-answers.md)).

## Not handled

- A gust is only this hero's. A friend's stroke or shot stirs nothing: neither is sent.
- Plants and loose items do not answer a gust yet. The grass's sway (living flora) is the first to take one.
- Only the railgun and swung tools push air. A nailer, a running drill and a thrown charge do not: the charge has its own blast.
