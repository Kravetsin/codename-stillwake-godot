## Context

The starting wreck was a long rectangle, and a player who broke its generator or ran out of its power could lose the first room's air, and with it every fire. The user wanted a ship that looks like
something, and no dead end, while a fire must keep needing air. The whole design and its later steps are in [scoop.md](../plans/scoop.md).

## Decision

- **A new world starts in the torn-off scoop of a scavenger:** jaws and a hold on the left, then a lock, the iron room, a lock and a landing on the right, where a hero steps out and catches at once.
  The map is a character a cell, in the same box as before, so the pod and the lock doors keep their cells.
- **The pod is a first generator.** It makes `Air.GeneratorRate` with no power and vents into its room. The room starts with nothing else in it.
- **It does so through one short pipe inside its own footprint,** drawn on the map, because air only travels by pipe networks and a pod on a network needs no rule of its own: a test map's pod without
  that pipe, and an old save's, stays as it was. A branch from it feeds a first vent elsewhere. That pipe refuses a cut (`pipes.fixed`): cut, its tube sold, there would be no way back, as a tube needs
  `firstMetal`, which needs a furnace, which needs this air.
- **One layer of real iron round the room and its locks, the rest scrap.** A player may take the ship apart for resources; they pay with their own hull.
- **The panel stayed on the roof at first;** [hold-piles](2026-10-31-hold-piles.md) moved its `photoPanel` into a pile.
- **The lamp moves to the `machinery` ring,** beside the burner: nothing before that gate makes power. Until then the pod's own pale light shows the room.
- **The original plates show their age, cold, nothing burning.** The user picked speckled rust, ghost chevrons and ice-rimmed shards on a pick page, on a frame of the game; `tools/ship-art/wreck/gen.js` paints them. Where they lie is `Wreck.Dress`, data read by the client only.
- **`wreckScrap`, a row with scrap's rules and no card, says a wall is still original,** because the client must tell it from a rebuilt one and the grid's material code is all it holds per wall. A saved list of cells would be a new saved field for a look. A wall built in its place is `scrap`, clean.
- **A torn cell keeps its tear only with the sides it was drawn for,** and draws no back wall while it does, so stars show through its cuts; the sim's back wall stays. A neighbour built or taken away makes it plain scrap with an ordinary edge.
- **What still moves lives on the tears** (picked: `jump`, `thread`, `wires`): three wires sway out of the keel, a fine arc jumps every 6-14 s at the long wire's tip and across the roof tear, a thin thread of vapour seeps at 6-9 px/s against a leak's 90-170. Each lasts while its tear shows and throws nothing off the screen. The arc is the game's own `Bolt.Fine`; the wires are a function of time, since `Rope` pins both ends; the thread is a wisp of light, not matter, because the dark of space would hide what the room must light.
- **The hold is space:** a hero floats in it on the tether, as outside. Sealing it is ordinary building and its reward is gravity, air and a floor.

## Consequences

- A gap one cell wide makes a room of what lies behind it, so a drawing's slits are filled and fins stand two apart.
- Meteors strike the right side first, and the iron there mostly stands: the first hour's hull is a little harder than scrap's. To watch in the playtest, with what taking the ship apart pays (about 500 chunks and 76 plates).
- The hold's piles came next: [hold-piles](2026-10-31-hold-piles.md).
- Old saves keep their wreck, undressed: they hold no `wreckScrap`, so no format bump. A hero can bump up to 5 px of visible nothing at a torn cell, which is a whole wall to physics; none lies on a route a hero must walk. The plan's back focus shows stars at a torn cell, whose back wall the sim still holds; a back wall placed there is refused as occupied.
- A wire can pass through what a hero builds under the keel. An arc already thrown finishes its 200 ms after its tear goes. A thread may start a few frames before a fast camera has drawn its tear. Old saves have no tears, so none of this.
